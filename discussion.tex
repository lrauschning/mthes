Comparing to pangenome graphs based on local-alignments and variation graphs, MPG differ in a number of key ways.
First, small variants not breaking synteny are not represented in the graph structure.
This is similar to a common filtering step used for variation graphs \cite{matthews24, hickey24}.
As the graph can be annotated with exact positions on each sample and aligments are available in the PSF file, these can be recovered in a fairly straightforward way.
Second, a MPG will never introduce a circle by collapsing repetitive sequences, and will also never collapse nonlocal nodes or produce nodes with many nonlocal connections.
Similarly, no connection can be made between nodes on different chromosomes, ensuring that chromosomes can always cleanly be separated out as independent subgraphs.
In fact, the emitted GFA file contains comments indicating the chromosome subgraphs.

At the same time, as syntenic alignments are found using minimap2's approximate and SyRI may also fall back to a heuristic approach in complex situations, the multisyntenic pangenome graph constructed by msyd may in pratice be less than fully collapsed, particularly in highly diverged or repetitive regions.
Approaches to further collapse the graph or filter out rare or small nodes may be useful in practice, in particular with regard to private nodes.

Which graph representation is ideal will of course depend on the nature of the dataset and scientific question.
The MPG differs from other current pangenome representations.
It has a more regular structure, and aims to capture large-scale structure over local events.
In the next section, I will outline a procedure using the regular structure of the MPG to define a pangenomic coordinate system termed PanCo, allowing genomic coordinates to be compared across different samples in a principled and consistent way.%\footnote{Note that a similar procedure is in fact used to ensure consistent sorting across samples during MPG construction.}.
This not only serves a direct purpose, but also demonstrates the advantages of a representation possessing strong structural guarantees, and illustrates how these can be used to derive efficient algorithms appropriate for modern genomics datasets.
