\section*{Multisyntenic Pangenome Graphs as a Genome Representation}

Comparing to pangenome graphs based on local-alignments and variation graphs, MPG differ in a number of key ways.
First, MPGs are acyclic by construction, which other leading approaches cannot guarantee.
This dramatically simplifies the graph structure, and allows the use of more efficient algorithms for many common operation.
%First, small variants not breaking synteny are not represented in the graph structure.
%This is similar to a common filtering step used for variation graphs \cite{matthews24, hickey24}.
%As the graph can be annotated with exact positions on each sample and aligments are available in the PSF file, these can be recovered in a fairly straightforward way.
%Second, a MPG will never introduce a circle by collapsing repetitive sequences, and will also never collapse nonlocal nodes or produce nodes with many nonlocal connections.
MPGs will also never have nonlocal connections, as all edges represent adjacency on at least one sample.
This also guarantees that chromosomes will always form disjoined subgraphs in the MPG.%, as there cannot ever be adjacency across chromosomes.
%Similarly, no connection can be made between nodes on different chromosomes, ensuring that chromosomes can always cleanly be separated out as independent subgraphs.
%In fact, the emitted GFA file contains comments indicating the chromosome subgraphs.
This forms a stark difference to how other pangenome graph representations, which especially in repetitive sequences often introduce cycles or nonlocal connections.% choose to draw edges and collapse nodes; 

MPGs respect and merge the ordering of all samples; as remarked in the proof of acyclicity, the $<$ comparator on any sample cannot be violated by the graph's successor-of relation, as $<$ is the transitive closure of the per-sample directed adjacency $\prec_i$.\\
By baking in these structural guarantees, the Pangenome Coordinate (PanCo) system»falls out« of the graph representation very naturally, with practically no additional overhead.
It reflects the advantages of a pangenome representation with strong theoretical guarantees, and allows easy navigation in the graph by maintaining some of the properties of genomic indices on linear genome references.\\
%PanCos are not fully stable towards adding additional samples to the pangenome; while the coresyn part of the PanCo will not change, assuming the added sample does not break coresynteny, the merasyntenic part of the PanCo will likely require recomputing.
Compare the ease at which a MPG can be indexed with PanCos to the difficulty of indexing nodes in a variation graph: the odgi sort command implements multiple cycle-breaking algorithms in order to then traverse the entire graph and assign a topological ordering, alongside a force-directed-layout based stochastic embedding approach\cite{guarracino22}.
Meanwhile, I am not aware of any indexing, including via external tools, of minigraph's or minigraph-cactus' representations beside arbitrarily assigned numeric node IDs.


In general, the MPG representation has stricter criteria for collapsing parts of the raw haplotypes than other pangenome graphs.
In order to be collapsed, regions have to not just align to another but form part of a syntenic whole-genome alignment in multiple samples.
Taking whole-genome synteny into account during graph construction is what allows the MPG to have a linear backbone and an acyclic structure without nonlocal connections and to remain acyclic.\\
While the MPG construction itself uses an exact algorithm, % However, the preprocessing steps for MPG const
its inputs in the form of alignments and synteny calls may fall back to heuristic procedures that may sometimes miss real synteny.
Minimap2's approximate alignment may fail in highly repetitive or significantly diverged regions, while SyRI's greedy heuristic may not always find the largest chain of alignments to call as a syntenic backbone.\\
This underlies the anecdotal observation that the MPG sometimes appears undercollapsed, that is that it may often be possible to further collapse the MPG as produced by the preprocessing pipeline and graph construction algorithm without violating any of its guarantees.
This is desirable, as it would lead to a more compact and easier-to-visualize representation, while keeping all of the MPG's strengths.
Strategies for achieving this are discussed in the next section.

Recently, ONT-Ultralong protocols suitable for plant samples have become available\cite{lu24}.
Using the protocol, up to 4-fold coverage of potato genomes with ultralong reads > \qty{200}{\kilo\bp} per flowcell could be achieved (Craig Dent, personal communication).
Given the sequencing requirement minima derived in the first section in Main, this should make it feasible to generate read datasets with sufficient information to, in theory, phase all but the largest homozygous regions of the potato genome.\\
Testing whether current algorithms can leverage this information for single-platform phased assembly of potato genomes will be interesting, either enabling the high-throughput generation of potato genomes, or providing an opportunity for the development of such tools\footnote{Of note, none of the leading assembly tools were designed from the ground up with ultralong reads in mind; there may be significant potential still left to obtain from these data.}.

Another clear next step is to investigate and visualize the multisyntenic pangenome graph representation of the potato dataset.
Behaviour of the representations about regions known to be structurally variable, such as introgressed regions and regions containing NLR genes would be a particular point of interest.
% msyd-cactus
It would further be interesting to see whether it is possible to use the minigraph-cactus pipeline to »spikify« the MPG graph, recovering small variants\cite{hickey24}.
As the file format is compatible and msyd graphs lack small variants similar to minigraph's output, in principle this should be possible, and may be a way to leverage the strengths of different approaches facilitated by a common ecosystem.\\
Another approach would be to use the fact that multisyntenic regions have exact sequence positions on all of their samples as well as alignments of these regions to construct a consensus sequence for each node.
A Centre*-based approximative MSA could be computed efficiently given that the sequences are already aligned to a common reference.
A consensus could be derived either by column-wise majority vote, or represented as a sequence profile using HMM-based alignment algorithms\cite{remmert12}.\\
While the cactus-based approach would be faster to develop and benefit from cactus' efficient MSA approach\cite{armstrong20}, the availability of a per-node MSA may be interesting for population genetic studies, and profile-based alignments have proven very efficient and sensitive in both DNA and protein alignment\cite{tempel12, pfam20}.

To reduce the degree of undercollapsing observed in the MPG, an improvement to the reliability of msyd's realignment functionality would likely be a major step.

The main limitation during the realignment step seems to be the alignment itself; in particular, minimap2 often reports no alignments, even when manual inspection reveals sequences across different haplotypes to be almost base-level identical.
Minimap2's minimizer approach works less well when aligning smaller sequences; additionally, spacers inserted during the extraction of sequences for realignment may disrupt minimap2's alignment\cite{minimapns}.
%is likely not the best choice for sensitively aligning smaller regions, and has in the past also proven to not always work well with the spacers msyd inserts into already annotated regions.
As no synteny can be called without any alignments, the regions are falsely reported as private.\\

While some degree of undercollapsing may be unavoidable while maintaining computational efficiency, it is still generally desirable to collapse regions showing multisynteny.

Two strategies are possible to reduce the occurence of undercollapsing.
Either to tune the pipeline of the graph construction's inputs, or to introduce an additional step after graph induction that further collapses the graph, somewhat similar to smoothxg's function in PGGB\cite{garrison22}.\\
Porting the realignment step to use a Dynamic Programming-based aligner, for example Parasail, may make it more reliable\cite{daily16}.
For short sequences, this should not significantly change runtime; for longer sequences, perhaps a fallback to minimap2 or another heuristic approach may still be desirable.
An alternative would be a naïve, length-based merging of haplotypes, similar to that used by Jasmine\cite{jasmine}. 
While this would be a very computationally efficient approach, it would likely fail to resolve nested variation, a key goal of modern pangenomic representations.\\
A postprocessing solution would require more conceptual innovation, especially to ensure that all collapses introduced by the postprocessing step reflect real, global synteny and do not violate the MPG guarantees.
%n all-against-all alignment of nodes would be a 
A starting point could be selecting nodes sharing a predecessor and/or successor, and collapsing them if their sequences align.
Forcing collapsed nodes to share an adjacent node would ensure that no cycles are introduced during the collapse operation -- to introduce a cycle by merging of two nodes in an acyclic graph, the two merged nodes must be successor/predecessor of another.\\

Exploring the consequences of the MPG genome representation more broadly... \\
Beyond an indexing scheme, the Pangenomic Coordinate system can be thought of as a linearization of the pangenome maintaining consistency with all input haplotypes as a sort of consensus genome structure of the samples.
If the samples are representative of the wider population, this linearization could be compared with other populations, both within and across species.
This could enable both analyses of how genome structure evolves across separated population, commonly thought to be a major driving force of speciation since early work on cryptic polymorphisms inducing reproductive incompatibility by Dobzhansky and Müller\cite{TODO}.\\
It could also provide a novel framework to perform intra-species association studies, if the populations are chosen as samples showing or not showing a certain trait.
To estimate the heritability of the trait, the consensus linearizations of the splits could be compared using e.g. whole-genome-alignment algorithms, and compared to random splits to account for population structure.
If this comparison can be made locally, it could also map associations to certain regions of the MPG.%Comparing the local similarity
