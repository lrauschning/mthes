Recently, Samija Amar in the lab managed to design a protocol to reliably obtain ultralong reads > \qty{200}{\kilo\bp} from potato.
According to the sequencing minima derived in the first section of the main, this should make it feasible to generate read datasets with sufficient information to, in theory, phase all but the largest homozygous regions of the potato genome.%generate be sufficient to 
Testing whether current algorithms can leverage this information for single-platform phased assembly of potato genomes will be interesting, either enabling the high-throughput generation of potato genomes, or providing an opportunity for the development of such tools\footnote{Of note, none of the leading assembly tools were designed from the ground up with ultralong reads in mind; there may be significant potential still left to obtain from these data.}.

Regarding msyd, during dataset construction the realignment functionality of msyd proved to not unreliable, particularly in the potato dataset.
This causes the multisyntenic pangenome graph to be undercollapsed in non-reference haplotypes.
Testing problematic cases revealed that minimap2 may not report any alignment in short sequences, likely as a result of not finding enough minimizers.
Porting the realignment step to use a Dynamic Programming-based aligner, for example Parasail, may make it more reliable\cite{daily16}.
For short sequences, this should not significantly change runtime; for longer sequences, perhaps a fallback to minimap2 or another heuristic approach may still be desirable.

Comparing to pangenome graphs based on local-alignments and variation graphs, MPG differ in a number of key ways.
First, small variants not breaking synteny are not represented in the graph structure.
This is similar to a common filtering step used for variation graphs \cite{matthews24, hickey24}.
As the graph can be annotated with exact positions on each sample and aligments are available in the PSF file, these can be recovered in a fairly straightforward way.
Second, a MPG will never introduce a circle by collapsing repetitive sequences, and will also never collapse nonlocal nodes or produce nodes with many nonlocal connections.
Similarly, no connection can be made between nodes on different chromosomes, ensuring that chromosomes can always cleanly be separated out as independent subgraphs.
In fact, the emitted GFA file contains comments indicating the chromosome subgraphs.

At the same time, as syntenic alignments are found using minimap2's approximate and SyRI may also fall back to a heuristic approach in complex situations, the multisyntenic pangenome graph constructed by msyd may in practice be less than fully collapsed, particularly in highly diverged or repetitive regions.
Approaches to further collapse the graph or filter out rare or small nodes may be useful in practice, in particular with regard to private nodes.

Which graph representation is ideal will of course depend on the nature of the dataset and scientific question.
The MPG differs from other current pangenome representations.
It has a more regular structure, and aims to capture large-scale structure over local events.
In the next section, I will outline a procedure using the regular structure of the MPG to define a pangenomic coordinate system termed PanCo, allowing genomic coordinates to be compared across different samples in a principled and consistent way.%\footnote{Note that a similar procedure is in fact used to ensure consistent sorting across samples during MPG construction.}.
This not only serves a direct purpose, but also demonstrates the advantages of a representation possessing strong structural guarantees, and illustrates how these can be used to derive efficient algorithms appropriate for modern genomics datasets.

The fact that this powerful and interpretable coordinate system »falls out« of the graph representation at practically no additional computational cost reflects the advantages of a pangenome representation with strong theoretical guarantees.
%PanCos are not fully stable towards adding additional samples to the pangenome; while the coresyn part of the PanCo will not change, assuming the added sample does not break coresynteny, the merasyntenic part of the PanCo will likely require recomputing.

Compare the ease at which a MPG can be indexed with PanCos to the difficulty of indexing nodes in a variation graph: the odgi sort command implements multiple cycle-breaking algorithms in order to then traverse the entire graph and assign a topological ordering, alongside a force-directed-layout based stochastic embedding approach\cite{guarracino22}.
Meanwhile, I am not aware of any indexing, including via external tools, of minigraph's representations beside arbitrarily assigned numeric node IDs.
