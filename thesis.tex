Assembling a complete genome from telomere to telomere has gone from being impossible to an effort requiring a well-resourced consortium to routine in the span of less than 5 years.
While for humans, high-quality complete genomes can now be obtained using a single sequencing platform to simplify the process, this has not been reported in other species.% the »recipe« to reliably obtain a high-quality, phased genome assembly is known through trial and error, substantial uncertainty remains in polyploids with less resources.
Particularly in polyploids, substantial ambiguity remains as to whether this is impossible in principle, and if sequencing technology could overcome this.
In this thesis, I derive a set of minimal requirements for phasing an assembly using ultralong reads.

The ease nowadays of assembling genomes promises a more complete understanding of genomic variation than previous resequencing efforts; yet it also lays bare that the foundations of the field are not built to accomodate modern assembly datasets

To adequately represent large assembly datasets, I propose the Multisyntenic Pangenome Graph (MPG).
%In this thesis, I propose a genome representation for assembly datasets.
The MPG is conceptually motivated as an extension of the pairwise theory of genomic synteny underlying the SyRI algorithm, and accounts for global genome structure when collapsing sequences into nodes.
This provides guarantees that other proposed representations do not, in particular it avoids cycles and nonlocal edges, and provides a linear backbone of shared structure across all samples.\\
The graph representation possesses a natural index that preserves some of the properties of linear genome indices.
This allows efficient description and navigation along the graph.
